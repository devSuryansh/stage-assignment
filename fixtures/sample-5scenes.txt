EXT. CENTRAL JAIL - MAIN GATE - DAY1


A high wall the colour of old bone, topped with broken
glass and rusted wire. A watchtower. A black iron gate
set into the wall like a mouth. Above it, faded paint: a
government seal, a motto no one reads.


SUPER: "पहला   दिन" / DAY ONE
A prison bus grinds to a halt. The gate groans open. It
swallows the bus and shuts.




INT. JAIL - INTAKE ROOM - DAY2


A bare room. A ceiling fan pushing hot air in circles. A
long counter, behind it a HEAD CLERK with a ledger and a
stamp pad. A line of new prisoners shuffles forward,
ankles loose, eyes on the floor. At the back — THE
CONVICT. Young. Head down. A cut over one eye, half-
healed. We do not see his full face. A HAVALDAR (45)
works the line — pot-bellied, unhurried, a man who has
processed ten thousand men and stopped seeing any of
them. He shoves each new arrival into place with the flat
of his hand. The Convict reaches the counter. The
Havaldar reads the file, then reads it again, slower.
Something in it makes his mouth curl.



                           HAVALDAR
                     (LOUD, TO THE ROOM)
          अरे देखो देखो। बड़ा माल आया है आज।


Heads turn. The Havaldar taps the file with one thick
finger.



                           HAVALDAR (CONT'D)
                     (READING, SAVOURING)
          अपहरण। बलात्कार। और खून।
                     (A LOW WHISTLE)
          तीनों एक साथ। पूरा पैके ज।


Laughter from a couple of trusties near the wall. The
Convict does not lift his eyes.



                           HEAD CLERK
                     (BORED, STAMP POISED)
          नाम?


The Convict opens his mouth. Nothing comes. His jaw
works.



                           HAVALDAR
                     (CUTTING IN,
                     CONTEMPTUOUS)
          नाम-वाम रहने दे। इनका नाम नंबर होता है।


He grabs a placard, a stub of chalk, scrawls a number
[613] on it, and slaps it against the Convict's chest.
                           HAVALDAR (CONT'D)
        आज से तू ये है। और कु छ नहीं।




INT. JAIL - INTAKE / STRIP AREA - CONTINUOUS3


A tin partition. The Convict's own clothes come off — his
shirt, folded once by his own hands with a strange care,
then thrown into a gunny sack. A coarse grey uniform
tossed at his feet. As he pulls off the shirt, his hand
closes for half a second around the collar — a specific
spot, a small hard lump sewn into the seam. He does not
look at it. He simply, quickly, transfers it. Before the
guard's eyes come back, his fingers have worked something
out of the old collar and pressed it into the fist he
keeps closed. We do not see what it is.



                           HAVALDAR (O.S.)
        जल्दी कर। तेरे   ࣽ लिए रेड कारपेट नहीं ࣺ बिछा है।
The Convict pulls on the grey shirt. The lump is gone —
somewhere on him now. His fist opens, empty and innocent.




INT. JAIL - CORRIDOR - CONTINUOUS4


The Havaldar marches him down a long gallery. Cell doors.
Faces at the bars, tracking the new meat.



                           HAVALDAR
                   (WALKING, ALMOST
                   FRIENDLY NOW)
        सुन। यहाँ का उसूल सीधा है। जो ऊपर बैठा है,
        वो भगवान। जो नीचे है, वो कीड़ा।
                   (A GLANCE BACK)
        और तू — तेरे के स के साथ — तू कीड़े से भी नीचे है।


They stop at a heavy barred door. Beyond it, the barrack.



                            HAVALDAR (CONT'D)
        बलात्कारी को यहाँ कोई पसंद नहीं करता। जेल
        में भी इज़्ज़त का   ࣹ हसाब होता है।
                    (SLIDING THE BOLT)
        भगवान भला करे तेरा।


He shoves the Convict in. The door clangs shut.




INT. JAIL - BARRACK - DAY5


A long hall. Maybe thirty men. Bedrolls in rows, a
stinking corner latrine, a barred window throwing bars of
light across the concrete. The heat sits on everything
like a hand. The room goes quiet. Thirty pairs of eyes.
At the far end, on a raised bedroll with an actual pillow
and a small tin trunk, sits the king of this place —
DAGDU (40s). Shirt open, a scar like a zip down one
forearm, a matchstick working between his teeth. He does
not get up. Power never has to. Beside the window, cross-
legged, an OLD MAN sits shelling something into a folded
cloth. GANPAT (60s) — spare, white-stubbled, calm as a
stone in a river. His eyes are the only ones in the room
that are not cruel — only tired, and watching. The
Convict stands just inside the door, number on his chest,
holding his rolled bedding. A TRUSTY at Dagdu's side
reads the whisper off the corridor grapevine and grins,
calling it out for the whole room.



                            TRUSTY
        दगडू भाई — नया वाला। बलात्कारी है। और एक लड़के
        को भी मारके आया है।
Dagdu rolls the matchstick to the other corner of his
mouth. He studies the Convict the long, lazy way a
butcher studies a hanging carcass.



                            DAGDU
                    (TO THE ROOM, NOT TO
                    HIM)
         हमारे घर भी बहन बेटियाँ हैं। ऐसे जानवर को हम अपने
         बीच नहीं रखते।


He points the matchstick at a spot on the bare floor by
the latrine corner — the worst place, where the smell
lives.



                            DAGDU (CONT'D)
         वहाँ। कीड़े के लायक जगह।


Nobody moves to help. The Convict walks to the corner and
sets his bedding down. Slowly. Eyes down.



                            GANPAT
                    (QUIETLY, WITHOUT
                    LOOKING UP FROM HIS
                    SHELLING)
         सुनी-सुनाई पे मत जा, दगडू । कागज़ पढ़ के
         कौन आता है यहाँ।



                            DAGDU
                    (AMUSED)
         गणपत काका। बूढ़े हो गए, पर दया अभी भी बची है।
                    (A HARD GRIN)
         कागज़ की ज़रूरत नहीं। चेहरा पढ़ लो, बस।


Ganpat says nothing more. He goes back to his shelling.
But his eyes flick, once, to the young man in the corner
— and hold there a moment longer than they need to.
