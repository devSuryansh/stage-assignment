EXT. SABZI MANDI - MAIN LANE - DAWN


Dust lifts off the brick lane before the sun clears the peepal.
Iron weighing scales. Heaps of green chillies. A loudspeaker
somewhere plays an old Haryanvi song too early. RAMU (50),
gamcha on his shoulder, arranges onions into a neat pyramid.
Beside him, a younger seller — BITTU (22) — scrolls his phone
and ignores the tomatoes.


                           RAMU
          अरे बिट्टू, तराजू साफ कर। धूल खा रही है।


                           BITTU
          कर लूँगा बाबा। अभी दो मिनट।


A WOMAN in a printed suit stops, eyes the onions, lifts one,
squeezes it, puts it back. Ramu watches without blinking.



INT. TEA STALL - MANDI CORNER - MORNING


A two-burner stove. Brass tumblers. The TEA BOY pours from
height. Ramu and Bittu sit on a wooden bench. Across from
them, HAVELI UNCLE (60) nurses a glass and a newspaper he
cannot read without glasses.


                           HAVELI UNCLE
          सुना? रात को किसी ने श्याम की भैंस खोली।


                           RAMU
          भैंस नहीं, दिमाग खोला होगा किसी का।


Bittu laughs too loud. The Tea Boy nearly spills.


                           BITTU
          बाबा, आज भाव बढ़ा दो प्याज़ का। शहर वाले आ रहे हैं।


                           RAMU
          शहर वाले आएँ या राजा, प्याज़ झूठ नहीं बोलती।



EXT. SABZI MANDI - PARKING EDGE - DAY


A scooter tipped on its side. Two men argue over a scratch
on a chrome mudguard. A LOST GIRL (6) stands holding a
plastic bag of coriander, crying without sound. Bittu sees
her first.


                           BITTU
          ओए छोरी, तेरी माँ कहाँ?


The Girl points vaguely toward the chilli heaps. Ramu puts
down his scale, walks over, and takes her hand as if it were
the most ordinary chore in the market.


                           RAMU
          चल, ढूँढते हैं। प्याज़ इंतज़ार कर लेगी।
